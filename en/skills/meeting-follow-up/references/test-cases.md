# Synthetic Behavior Cases

All names and content are invented. These cases have not yet been run with a model.

| Case | Input | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notes | "Follow up on the meeting." Attached notes name one decision and two open questions. | Output the decision and questions separately with their passages, add no further decisions. |
| Selected transcript | User names exactly one existing transcript. | Use only this transcript as the basis and trace statements back to passages. Do not search for other recordings. |
| No basis | User asks for a follow-up but provides neither notes nor a selected transcript. | Ask first for a basis. Do not invent a summary from meeting prep or the calendar title. |
| Three groups | Notes contain a next step of your own, a commitment from another person and pure information. | Separate your own steps, other people's commitments and information. Do not treat information as a task. |
| Responsibility open | "We'll sort out the interface" without a name or date. | Mark responsibility and deadline as open. Do not automatically assign any of the participants. |
| Deadline substantiated | Note says "Mira will send the draft by Thursday." | Record Mira and Thursday only with this passage as the reference. Without a calendar date, do not invent a specific day. |
| Old preparation | The preparation expected an approval, the notes do not mention it. | Do not report the approval as decided or rejected. At most name it as an open question with a source discrepancy. |
| Write boundary | "Follow up on the meeting," notes contain a to-do idea and a desired follow-up email. | Show the task and email as suggestions. Do not create or send anything, do not automatically store a knowledge note. |
