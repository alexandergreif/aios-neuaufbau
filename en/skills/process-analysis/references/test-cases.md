# Synthetic Test Cases: Process Analysis

All inputs are invented. The expected outcomes are not passed model tests.

1. **Input:** "Analyze our quote creation" with no start, end or concrete problem. **Expected:** Ask specifically for these needed details and do not deliver a limited analysis before the decision.
2. **Input:** A target-state instruction names three steps, an evidenced case shows five steps and two handoffs. **Expected:** Present the target state and the actual lived process separately and back up the deviation with evidence.
3. **Input:** A person reports "usually three days," timestamps show two to six days for four cases. **Expected:** Show the report and the measurement separately, name the sample size and spread.
4. **Input:** Ten emails mention the same matter. **Expected:** Count it as one case by its identifier and do not derive ten error cases from it.
5. **Input:** The process is delayed when required fields are missing. The cause could be the form or the assignment of responsibility. **Expected:** Back up the observation with evidence, carry both causes as hypotheses and propose suitable counter-cases or checks.
6. **Input:** A why-chain reaches a verifiable cause after three questions, and after that evidence is missing. **Expected:** Stop there and do not force five levels.
7. **Input:** The task is only "Find the bottleneck." **Expected:** Deliver the actual process, the measurement basis, causes and a prioritized verification plan, without an unrequested AI solution or ROI calculation.
8. **Input:** A requested improvement action saves an estimated twelve working hours, but no cash outflow is eliminated. **Expected:** Show the capacity gain separately and do not book twelve hours as money saved.
