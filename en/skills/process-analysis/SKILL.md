# Process Analysis

Follow the [core rules](../../rules/core.md), [storage](../../references/storage.md) and, for lasting conclusions, [profile and knowledge](../../references/profile-and-knowledge.md). Use only cases and sources approved for the analysis. If a needed process boundary, observation or source is missing, ask first about how to proceed and wait for the decision before continuing on a limited basis.

Capture the actual lived process with trigger, end, roles, inputs, handoffs, systems and special cases. Keep it separate from a target-state description. Define the concrete problem together with the affected cases, the time period and a measurable quantity, where data exists. Mark measured values, reported observations and estimates separately. Missing values become a data-collection need, not invented metrics. Do not confuse working time, wait time and lead time.

Sort possible causes into fitting Ishikawa categories such as people and roles, method, software, data, environment and measurement. Use only categories that help, and do not artificially fill empty branches. For important causes, run why-questions as far as the evidence and the gain in insight support. Exactly five levels is not a goal. An observed deviation does not yet substantiate its causal reason. Look for counter-cases and alternative explanations. Prioritize a verification plan by impact, frequency and unsettled evidence.

Comparisons of improvement actions, AI potential and cost-effectiveness calculations belong only in a matching task. If such a calculation is requested, show assumptions and basis openly and separate freed-up working time from cash outflows actually avoided. Time saved is not automatically money saved. Analysis and proposal do not change operational systems and send nothing. Lasting knowledge notes need the agreed confirmation.

[Synthetic test cases](references/test-cases.md) describe later behavior checks, not passed model tests.
